### Candidate 5 — test_bdm_partitions asserts GPT partitions far beyond the device mount successfully, encoding RIPTOPL-160's missing extent validation as correct

- **Category:** 6 (bugs/security) — cross-reference RIPTOPL-160; this candidate files the *test*, not the production bug.
- **Locations:** `RiptOPL/.github/scripts/test_bdm_partitions.py:91,136` (simulated device is exactly 64 sectors), `:238-246` (entry at LBA `0x100000010` asserted connected), `:248-253` (entry at LBA 3000–3009 asserted connected, with the rationalising comment at `:249`); production check it normalises at `RiptOPL/modules/bdm/src/part_driver_gpt.c:130` and `:172-175`.
- **Problem:** RIPTOPL-160 (round 5, READ there) established that the GPT parser never validates an entry's extent against the device: the only sanity check compares the entry against the header's own disk-supplied usable range, and the resulting `sectorCount = last_lba - first_lba + 1` becomes the pseudo-device's I/O fence. Two scenarios in this test assert exactly that missing validation as expected behaviour on a device whose `sectorCount` is exactly 64: an entry beginning at `0x100000010` must connect (`:245`), and an entry at 3000–3009 must connect (`:253`) — the latter with the comment "Only the entry sectors are read at mount, so partitions may lie beyond the simulated disk", which treats a harness artefact (the mock device does not model partition-data reads at mount) as permission for the production behaviour. Unlike the MBR case in the same file (`:194-201`, dissolved below), no saturation or reporting exception is documented for GPT: the header's ranges and `bd->sectorCount` are both 64-bit, and the authors state their exceptions explicitly when they intend one. If RIPTOPL-160 is ever fixed, these two assertions fail for the wrong reason and pressure the fixer to keep the hole; until then they certify it.
- **Fix in words:** In the two scan-bounding scenarios, either give the simulated device a `sectorCount` that covers the entries (the mock's `sectorCount` field is independent of its 64-sector backing array, and entry-array reads stay at LBA 2–3) or drop the mount-success assertion and assert only the scan bound (`max_read_sector`), which is what those scenarios were written to prove.
- **Justification if the fix adds anything:** It adds nothing; it changes fixture geometry / removes one assertion per scenario.
- **Benefit:** The GPT scan-bounding coverage survives a future extent-validation fix, and the suite stops blessing partitions that lie wholly outside the device they were parsed from.
- **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low · **Confidence:** High that the test asserts beyond-device mounts (READ — arithmetic is direct from `:91,136,242-253`); Med that no undocumented GPT saturation exception justifies them (INFERRED from the file's own practice of documenting the MBR exception)
